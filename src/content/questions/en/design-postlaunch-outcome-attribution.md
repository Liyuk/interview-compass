title: How do you judge a redesign when the target metric does not move after launch?
question: After a redesign of an important flow, the expected completion rate does not improve. Support requests decline, existing users respond more positively, but new users behave worse. How would you check metric definitions and user segments, determine what effect the design had, and choose the next action?
intent: Assesses whether the candidate can interpret mixed post-launch evidence with causal discipline rather than labeling a design successful or unsuccessful from one aggregate metric.
signals:
  - Checks the target metric, observation window, segments, and comparability before and after launch
  - Treats behavioral data, qualitative feedback, and support cost as distinct evidence with stated limits
  - Proposes follow-up analysis or experiments that test a specific mechanism and inform a design decision
  - Identifies the stakeholders, constraints, and risks relevant to differences between lab performance and real-world outcomes.
  - Proposes evidence or a review point that could confirm or change the decision.
