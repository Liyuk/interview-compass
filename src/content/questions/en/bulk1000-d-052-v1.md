question: >-
  The business asks to “improve customer experience” without a clear pain point or deadline. How would you
  decompose it into verifiable problems, workstreams, and milestones?

  Follow-up: If a critical dependency slips by one cycle, how would you re-sequence the critical path, reset
  commitments, and reduce waiting?
intent: >-
  Assesses whether the candidate can turn a goal into owned actions, dependencies, checkpoints, and acceptance
  conditions while keeping delivery controlled through change. This prompt focuses on decomposing an ambiguous
  commitment into verifiable outcomes, with a follow-up about a critical dependency slips by one cycle.
signals:
  - >-
    Decomposing an ambiguous commitment into verifiable outcomes：Breaks the goal into accepted work, owners,
    and prerequisites
  - Surfaces capacity, timing, risk, and cross-team commitment conflicts early
  - Defines checkpoints, escalation paths, and outcome handoff
  - Defines affected users, tasks, and current baseline
  - Stages discovery, experiment, implementation, and outcome verification
  - Clarifies the definition, baseline, scope, and exclusions
  - Breaks down failure sources and identifies evidence
  - Separates immediate containment, process changes, and longer-term investment
  - Sets validation measures and review points
  - Separates goal, assumptions, and tasks instead of listing features
  - Names the unknowns that must be settled before starting
  - Makes the first step verifiable within a short cycle
  - Asks about target users, specific tasks, baseline, and impact first.
  - Separates outcome goals, deliverables, and unvalidated hypotheses.
  - Makes scope, dependencies, acceptance evidence, and open questions explicit.
