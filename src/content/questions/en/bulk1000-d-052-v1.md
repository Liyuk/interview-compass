title: 'Decomposing an ambiguous commitment into verifiable outcomes: A critical dependency slips by one cycle'
question: >-
  The business asks to “improve customer experience” without a clear pain point or deadline. How would you
  decompose it into verifiable problems, workstreams, and milestones?

  Follow-up: If a critical dependency slips by one cycle, how would you re-sequence the critical path, reset
  commitments, and reduce waiting?
intent: >-
  Assesses whether the candidate can turn a goal into owned actions, dependencies, checkpoints, and acceptance
  conditions while keeping delivery controlled through change. This prompt focuses on decomposing an ambiguous
  commitment into verifiable outcomes, with a follow-up about a critical dependency slips by one cycle.
signals:
  - >-
    Clarify affected users, tasks, pain points, baseline, and timing, separating facts, outcomes, and
    untested hypotheses.
  - >-
    Break discovery, experiments, implementation, and outcome validation into short, testable steps
    rather than a feature list.
  - >-
    Identify dependencies, actual owners, capacity, and acceptance evidence for each step, retaining
    unconfirmed conditions.
  - >-
    When a critical dependency slips, compare sequencing, scope, and timing options and clarify authority
    to recommit and communicate impacts.
  - >-
    Set checkpoints, escalation conditions, and stop or adjustment signals, verifying user outcomes
    rather than task completion alone.
