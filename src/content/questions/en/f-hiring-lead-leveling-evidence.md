title: When leveling a lead candidate and setting aside title, company name and interview confidence, what do you look at instead?
question: When leveling a lead candidate, if you do not mainly rely on past titles, company names, or confidence in the interview, what evidence do you look at? Which "things not done" do you probe, and how do you separate fact, inference, and personal preference?
intent: See whether you judge level by the complexity of responsibility a person has carried, and can keep fact, inference, and preference apart so the same responsibility is not measured by different yardsticks.
signals:
  - Asks what responsibility they held at what complexity, how they explain their trade-offs, how results were verified, and which costs they saw and dealt with
  - Probes what was not done, such as what was given up when resources fell short, who bore the cost when teams disagreed, and whether they adjusted goals, organization, or people first when results missed
  - Tells apart being a strong deliverer, having many relationships, and being able to connect judgment, delegation, resources, and relationships into results
  - Writes disagreements as concrete questions rather than closing with "poor culture fit" or "not mature enough", and says final level is approved through the organization's authority mechanism
