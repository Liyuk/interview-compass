title: A candidate says "I care about code quality" or "I love learning". As an interviewer, how do you probe to tell a slogan from a habit?
question: A candidate says "I care about code quality" or "I love learning". As an interviewer, how do you probe to tell whether it is a slogan or a real habit, and how do you do it without making the candidate feel picked on?
intent: See whether you can probe a general self-assessment into specific behavior that can be checked while staying fair to the candidate.
signals:
  - Asks for recent specific examples, such as concrete changes made, code read, and what was learned
  - Checks with details, such as the problem, choices, and results at the time, rather than defeating them with obscure knowledge
  - Separates "has no concrete example" from "expresses it badly" and gives a chance to add
  - Uses consistent probing across candidates and records the specific behavior observed

followUps:

  - The candidate truly has no such experience but shows strong willingness to learn. How do you assess that?
