title: How do you judge growth quality when gross margin is stable but service mix changes?
question: >-
  Gross margin is steady, but standard-service revenue falls while custom-service revenue grows, along with
  implementation and support hours. How would you determine whether growth is healthy?
intent: "Assess whether you can look through stable aggregate margin to detect changes in service mix, delivery load, and reusable capability, then turn growth-quality evidence into observable product and operating choices."
signals: ["Break net revenue, direct and shared labor, implementation time, reuse, and support load down by service tier and customer segment; state the allocation basis.", "Check whether high-margin tiers or a few large customers mask costs, separating short-term delivery expense from ongoing maintenance obligations.", "Connect mix to capacity, customer outcomes, renewal, and cash needs; distinguish correlation from growth drivers.", "Compare limiting customization, repricing, productization, or service delivery with testable transition criteria."]
weakAnswer: "Citing stable aggregate margin or evenly allocating all support costs without validating service-level workload, reuse, and future maintenance obligations."
anchors:
  notPassing: "Treats stable aggregate margin as proof of healthy growth without examining mix or cost."
  passing: "Segments revenue and key labor by service and customer and identifies at least one potentially hidden cost."
  excellent: "Explains shared-cost basis, capacity and renewal effects, separates evidence from inference, and compares operating choices."
  exceptional: "Uses ranges and sensitivity analysis to support portfolio decisions, with validation thresholds, service commitments, and exit criteria for productizing custom work."
