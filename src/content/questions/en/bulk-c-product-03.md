title: Users repeatedly complain about a problem, but few change their behavior. How would you interpret that?
question: >-
  Interviews and support tickets repeatedly mention the same problem, but few users in a pilot are willing to
  change their current workflow. How would you determine whether the issue is how the need was expressed,
  switching cost, target-user selection, or problem priority?
intent: >-
  Assess whether the candidate can explain several reasons why stated feedback and actual behavior diverge,
  then choose a next step that reduces uncertainty.
signals:
  - Separates complaint frequency, severity, current workarounds, and willingness to change
  - Segments users by task, context, or constraint instead of hiding variation in an aggregate
  - Chooses a low-cost observation or test that can distinguish competing explanations
  - Observes behavior through real goals and neutral tasks instead of asking whether users like it.
  - Separates comprehension, willingness to try, current alternatives, and adoption costs.
  - Records counterevidence such as silence, workarounds, and misunderstanding, then updates hypotheses.
