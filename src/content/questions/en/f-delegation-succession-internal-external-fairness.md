title: A key lead may leave next year. How do you decide between developing internally and hiring externally, and how do you allocate succession opportunities fairly?
question: A lead who carries key responsibility may leave next year. How do you decide between developing someone internally, hiring externally, or a mix of both? How do you allocate succession opportunities fairly, and look after both those not chosen and those who step in temporarily?
intent: See whether you treat succession as preparing real opportunities for the next transfer of responsibility rather than naming a backup, and keep the criteria open and explainable.
signals:
  - Starts by listing which responsibilities only one person can carry and which context lives in one head, then decides which capabilities are worth growing through real work
  - Combines internal development and external hiring according to the problem and time window, rather than treating "outsiders are stronger" or "insiders are more loyal" as fixed answers
  - Allocates opportunities by open, explainable criteria, promises promotion to no one in advance, and gives those covering temporarily the support and recognition they need for the extra work
  - Lets people who were not chosen know how to get feedback and the next opportunity, rather than leaving them labeled "not suited"
