title: As an interviewer, how do you ask uncomfortable questions without crossing a line?
question: As an interviewer, how do you ask uncomfortable questions without crossing a line? When would you deliberately apply pressure, and how do you make sure the questions relate to the role, the candidate is respected, and the intent is explained afterwards?
intent: See how, as an interviewer, you tell a role-grounded probe from mere hostility, and protect both the candidate's dignity and the fairness of the judgment.
signals:
  - Says that pressure must correspond to an ability the role truly needs, such as handling challenge, not to display authority
  - Separates sharp probing from personal attack, and never uses the latter
  - Uses consistent questions for every candidate and records observed behavior rather than impressions
  - Explains the intent to the candidate at the end and leaves room to clarify
