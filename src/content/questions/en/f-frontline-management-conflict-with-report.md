title: A direct report comes into conflict with you. How do you handle it?
question: "A direct report openly disagrees with one of your decisions or raises concerns about your management. How would you understand the disagreement, assess its evidence, and decide what to change or uphold?"
intent: See how you treat conflict from a report, whether you listen to what is reasonable in it, and whether you hold a decision when needed.
signals:
  - First works out the specific point of disagreement and the basis behind it, rather than getting defensive
  - Separates reasonable feedback from plain opposition and says what the judgment rests on
  - Says what can be adjusted as a result and what must stand, with the reason
  - Repairs the relationship afterwards and checks whether the conflict exposed a management problem

weakAnswer: Treats a report’s disagreement as insubordination and suppresses it before checking the evidence.

  notPassing: Demands compliance or suppresses conflict without hearing the specific objection.

  exceptional: Checks the relationship and process afterwards; corrects and repairs openly if the judgment was wrong.
