title: A candidate's project and results sound impressive. How do you probe to tell packaging from real change?
question: A candidate's project and results sound impressive. How do you probe to tell whether they are packaging the project and its output or whether it truly made a real difference?
intent: See whether, as an interviewer, you can use checkable follow-ups to tell "told well" from "actually done", without assuming bad faith up front.
signals:
  - Asks how the numbers are defined, what they are compared against, and over what period, rather than accepting adjectives
  - Verifies with specifics, such as the constraints then, the trade-offs made, and the problems met
  - Gives the candidate a chance to clarify, and does not treat "cannot explain" as "made up"
  - Separates "no evidence" from "evidence against"

  - How do you avoid being carried along by fluency?
