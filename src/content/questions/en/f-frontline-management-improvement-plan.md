title: How do you create a fair improvement plan when someone repeatedly misses role expectations?
question: A team member’s recent work has repeatedly fallen below role expectations, and earlier feedback has not led to improvement. How would you create and carry out a fair, measurable performance improvement plan?
intent: See whether you can make performance improvement a sincere, supported process rather than a formality before an exit, and keep it fair and compliant.
signals:
  - Compare concrete facts with communicated role expectations and check goals, resources, and support, separating performance gaps from management conditions.
  - Agree observable, time-bound improvements and support without predetermining failure.
  - Set regular two-way feedback and records, explaining possible next decisions and their evidence in advance.
  - Handle formal decisions with HR under actual authority and organizational process; specialist coaching does not confer personnel authority.

weakAnswer: Uses the improvement plan as an exit form, sets goals retroactively, and does not document support or feedback.
anchors:
  notPassing: Sets vague or retroactively raised targets and treats the plan as a prelude to dismissal.
  passing: Documents gaps against role expectations and checks the employee’s resources and support.
  excellent: Agrees observable goals, deadlines, feedback cadence, and responsibilities on both sides.
  exceptional: Reviews consistent evidence, adjusts goals affected by dependencies, and follows formal process for decisions.
