title: The core business has peaked and you are sent to a new, unfamiliar, giant-dominated market. How do you define its north-star metric in the first thirty days?
question: The company's core business has peaked and growth is slowing. The boss sends you to lead a team exploring a completely blank, even giant-dominated, new market. In your first thirty days, how do you use market sizing and reasoning about the industry's end state to define this new business's north-star metric?
intent: See how you form judgment in a market without precedent through estimation and reasoning and set checkable conditions for that judgment.
signals:
  - Explains how the market size is estimated, the key assumptions, and the range of uncertainty, not only one large number
  - Reasons about the industry's end state and the giants' advantages and finds their own possible entry point
  - Ties the north-star metric to validating that entry point rather than using a headline metric
  - Sets in advance what result would show the direction does not hold
