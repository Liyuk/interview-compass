title: When you explain a business, how do you cover the whole first and then the part you own?
question: "When explaining a business, how do you describe the whole system first, then locate the part you own and explain its value and limits?"
intent: See whether you can build the whole picture first and then place your own work inside it, rather than describing only your own module.
signals:
  - Uses a few sentences to give the whole business first, covering who the users are, how value arises, and the main stages
  - Says where the part you own sits in the whole, and what it depends on and serves
  - Says what that part does for the overall result and what it is not responsible for
  - Is candid about the parts of the whole they do not understand

weakAnswer: Uses a list of technical modules as the business overview and cannot connect personal work to a user or outcome.
anchors:

  passing: Explains the business goal and the part personally owned.
  excellent: Connects upstream and downstream dependencies, outcome measures, and the scope of personal influence.
  exceptional: Identifies unverified links in the causal path and information outside personal knowledge.
