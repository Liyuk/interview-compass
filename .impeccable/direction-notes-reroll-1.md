# Interview Book visual direction round 2

This is a fresh hand after the user re-rolled. All worlds from round 1 are excluded, including the accession register, editorial handbook, museum provenance, film bench, transit atlas, Bauhaus, and radio running order.

The product remains a bilingual question library. Its unit is a prompt plus its stated purpose. Readers enter by search or topic browsing and can explore roles and responsibility contexts. The design must not present answers, scoring, a single reader journey, or company-specific claims. Starlight constrains documentation structure; Radix Themes/Primitives constrain visual tokens and accessible interactions.

The predictable category rut remains the generic centered-hero docs site with a grid of rounded cards. These seven candidates are intentionally drawn from different visual traditions:

1. **Conversation prompt deck** — a set of open questions organized by topic, with the purpose printed alongside each prompt. This is the closest everyday object to the product; risk: a literal grid of cards is a familiar UI cliché.
2. **Open standards index** — numbered entries, concise abstracts, status and cross references from standards documents. It makes stable IDs and version context legible; risk: it can skew too technical.
3. **Periodic table of professional questions** — a compact matrix groups prompts under the primary capability each explores, while search and role filters remain independent. It communicates the taxonomy at a glance; risk: a table may imply the collection is fixed or force every question into one shallow cell.
4. **Botanical identification field guide** — a classification key links observations to a named entry and further reading. It supports exploration without an answer key; risk: its natural-history subject can become a decorative costume.
5. **Question field guide** — use the field guide's clear taxonomy, key, and observation notes for a professional reference: topic and role keys lead to a question, and its intent is the observation note. Risk: it may feel like a training manual unless search stays first-class.
6. **Annotated score** — questions are independent movements with margin notes on intent and related themes. This carries a clear reading rhythm; risk: sequencing can imply a fixed interview script.
7. **Audio equipment signal map** — signal paths and labeled controls show where different question families connect. It can make cross-links visible; risk: it can overfit engineering readers and suggest a complex tool.

The second roll assigned candidate 5, the Question Field Guide. The Conversation Prompt Deck is the strongest familiar grounded pick and is offered as an alternate. The Design Annual Plate Section and Star Atlas challengers remain full alternatives: one contributes disciplined attribution/grid structure; the other makes cross-links and independent exploration visible. The remaining challengers are declined because they reduce audience identification or product clarity; each contributes one named discipline below.

Assigned direction: **The Question Field Guide**. Keep Starlight's restrained documentation skeleton and Radix's spacing, color, type, and control rules. Use a warm neutral background, ink text, one olive or muted teal navigation accent and a quiet signal red for current location; no faux plant illustrations or paper texture. First view: Interview Book header, Chinese/English switch, visible search, a concise subject key for the eight capability domains, then a featured question record. Its title and intent appear together; related topics and role links are secondary. The left key can become a compact native selector on narrow screens. Risk: a field guide metaphor can read like an instruction manual; keep the title and direct search literal.

Retained strengths from the competitive alternatives:

- **Evidence attribution** (Design Annual Plate): show source/status metadata only when present, visually separate from question intent.
- **Coordinate cross-links** (Star Atlas): make role/topic/capability cross-links direct and queryable without drawing a literal star chart.

Named raises from declined challengers:

- **Plain-language first** (Hand-drawn Zine): keep illustrative explanatory content out; use plain prompts and readable purpose labels.
- **Explicit state labels** (Emission Line Rail): filters and current selections have visible names and text state, not color-only encoding.
- **Reversible navigation** (Alphabet Storm): topic exploration can always return to the stable question index; no transformative text effects.
- **No commerce metaphor** (Sneaker Archive Wall): avoid inventory, variant, and ownership cues that do not describe a question.

Challenge comparison against the assigned direction:

| Challenger | Audience identification | Product clarity | Verdict | Useful discipline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Design annual's plate section | Medium: editorial reference readers recognize the print grammar | High: records and attribution sit together | Competitive | Full alternate; the assigned field guide retains optional source metadata as a separate note |

The canon exit is a conventional documentation/question-index site using ordinary search, sidebar, and document detail pages, shown as a valid neutral choice rather than a recommendation.
