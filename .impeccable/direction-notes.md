# Interview Book visual direction round

Product truth: a bilingual question library. The durable unit is one question with its purpose and optional reading context. Readers enter through direct search or topic browsing, then can explore by role, capability, responsibility scope, or interview/promotion context. The site should feel like a useful professional reference to candidates, interviewers, and people preparing promotion evidence.

The category rut to avoid is a generic documentation landing page with a centered slogan, repeated rounded cards, gradients, and a search box pasted above a link grid. Starlight is the approved reading/navigation framework and Radix Themes/Primitives constrain color, type, spacing, component states, and interactions. Any visual direction must preserve these constraints and keep the question and its purpose unmistakable.

Seven grounded candidate systems, ordered by resonance:

1. **Editorial interview handbook** — a well-edited technical reference opens with a useful index, clear section hierarchy, and questions annotated with what they explore. It is highly legible and familiar; risk: it can become another generic docs template.
2. **Public transit route atlas** — distinct colored lines can represent capability domains while stations connect topic and role paths. It makes cross-linking visible; risk: a fixed route metaphor can imply a required journey.
3. **Museum study-room accession register** — each question is a uniquely identified record with subject headings, context, provenance, and cross references. This supports stable IDs and source-aware browsing without turning the record into an answer. Risk: archive formality can feel cold.
4. **Film editor’s select bench** — questions are frames on a perforated rail; kept, deferred, and current selections remain legible. It can show how people curate a set; risk: it overemphasizes sequencing and adds interaction the product does not need.
5. **Newsroom query desk** — a question is edited for clarity, then linked to the people and topics it concerns. It has an accessible editorial voice; risk: it can resemble a publishing magazine more than a reusable question bank.
6. **Bauhaus foundation workshop** — visible grid, primary forms, and carefully structured exercises make decomposition concrete. Risk: arranging shapes is not the reader’s task and the product is not a design exercise.
7. **Radio studio running order** — numbered cue rows, transitions, and timing build a live conversation from distinct segments. It recognizes interview as a spoken exchange; risk: it can imply one correct sequence and hide self-guided browsing.

The roll assigned candidate 3, the study-room accession register. This is the lead, not a predetermined user choice. The editorial handbook is the strongest familiar pick and is shown as an alternate. Film select bench and provenance ribbon remain full challengers because each provides a distinct useful discipline. The other dealt challengers—Bauhaus workshop, street-fair flyer, data-sublime field, and origami sequence—are declined because they trail on both audience identification and product clarity; each still contributes one named discipline below.

Assigned direction: **The Question Accession Register**. The first screen is an operational index, not a promotional hero: language switch and global navigation at top, a wide bilingual-aware question search beside a concise topic index, then one featured question rendered as an accession record with its intent visibly attached. Browsing continues through subject headings and cross references. Palette/material: warm archival white, near-black text, one muted vermilion annotation color, restrained mineral blue for links; no faux paper texture. Type/composition: readable Starlight document scale, stable left index rail on wide screens, plain editorial column, metadata in compact monospace. Interaction: Radix dialog search, Radix accessible menus and focus states; links remain real URLs. Responsive: index rail becomes a labelled topic selector and question records keep their type scale. Risk: archive tone can imply hidden or dusty material; keep the live search, current bilingual label, and direct question links prominent.

Named raises from declined challengers:

- **Grid discipline** (Bauhaus workshop): use one consistent spacing/alignment grid and stable type hierarchy across the index, question record, and detail page; do not borrow its workshop tasks or primary-color palette.
- **Emphasis budget** (street-fair flyer): reserve one strong visual emphasis for the search and featured question; keep the rest quiet so the page remains readable, without importing its collage, marquee, or parallax.
- **Density control** (data-sublime field): use compact alignment for IDs and facets, while question text remains comfortably readable; do not use strobing, inversion, or monochrome as a theme.
- **Recoverable states** (origami sequence): every question and selected filter remains a stable, directly linkable state; do not make browsing a mandatory fold-by-fold sequence.

Challenge comparison (judged against the assigned register on audience identification and product clarity):

| Challenger | Audience identification | Product clarity | Verdict | Discipline raised/kept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Film editor’s select bench | Medium: editorial curation is recognizable, but interview readers do not edit film | Medium: rails communicate selection, but not directly the question’s purpose | Competitive | Full alternate; can contribute durable keep/defer states if later supported by a real reader task |
| Museum provenance ribbon | Low-medium: source-conscious authors recognize it, broad readers less so | Medium: traceability reads clearly, but provenance is not the primary task | Competitive | Full alternate; assigned direction keeps explicit source and adaptation metadata |
| Bauhaus foundation workshop | Low: the design-school exercise is remote from interview preparation | Low-medium: arrangement hides question discovery | Declined | Raised its grid discipline as named above |
| Street-fair flyer | Medium: public notices are familiar, but the crowded event idiom is not a question-library norm | Low: competing collage and motion would compete with question/intent | Declined | Raised one clear emphasis for search and featured question |
| Data-sublime field | Medium for technical readers, low for product/operations readers | Low: barcode density obscures question text and purpose | Declined | Raised compact metadata alignment while keeping prose readable |

The canon exit is a straightforward professional question-library/documentation page with normal search, topic navigation, and typography, kept subordinate to the grounded concepts. It exists as a valid choice, not a recommendation.
